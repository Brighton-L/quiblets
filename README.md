# Quiblets

A cozy 3D creature-expedition game built with Godot 4.7.

## Play

Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7 and press **Run Project**.

- Start with a single level-6 Plip and explore a low-poly 3D base-camp diorama while managing a team of 1–5 Quiblets, inspecting the always-accessible resources, training, and cooking.
- Every navigation back button sits in the bottom-right corner of the screen. The Quiblet info and All Quiblets menus draw over a full-screen backdrop.
- Cooking uses ingredient tags to produce deterministic dishes. Better quality improves arriving Quiblets; leftovers can be recycled or reinvested.
- Expeditions take place across 3D terrain. Click the ground to direct the whole team.
- Click the move icons on each Quiblet's card to activate moves manually. Enemies choose their own moves. Every move has an independent cooldown.
- Pause to inspect collected loot, resume, or **Give Up** and keep what you collected.
- Rare pickups sparkle during their collection animation. Tier-3 ingredients, every Move Stone, and Power Stones of tier 3 or with a bonus get gold sparkles; tier-4 ingredients and Power Stones of tier 4 or Silver quality and above get a brighter two-tone burst with a glow. The rule lives in `reward_sparkle_level` in `scripts/game_data.gd`; regression check `res://tests/reward_sparkles.gd`.

Each expedition island opens an eight-node route: three levels, a Berry Grove, two more levels, a Boss level, and an optional Berry Grove. Only reached nodes are playable; victories unlock the next node and cleared nodes remain replayable. Every node shows team-relative difficulty information and builds a deterministic map specific to its area and route position. Berry Groves are wide fields of five meadows packed with patches (26 in the Berry Grove, 34 in the optional one); a grove ends in victory the moment every patch is gathered, whether or not enemies remain, and only a few guardians wait in every other meadow, each two levels above a regular enemy with 25% more HP. Boss levels play like regular levels but on a larger field with seven to nine enemy sets instead of four to six, one extra enemy in every set, and three large arenas; the boss that follows the last set is much tougher (four times the HP, 1.7× damage, five extra levels, and a larger model) and fights alongside its escorts. Every special item can be used. Resources shows a USE button beside each owned item that targets a Quiblet (Health and Attack Charms, Memory Fruit, Move Crystal, Echo Crystal, Growth Fruit, Prodigy Fruit); the USE screen lists targets on the left, the item's choices and a preview on the right, and refuses impossible uses with a reason. Memory Fruit restores a forgotten move into a free slot or replaces a chosen one (its stones return to the inventory); Move Crystal adds a Move Stone slot up to eight; Echo Crystal copies a fitted Move Stone into the inventory; Growth Fruit levels a Quiblet up to its teammates' average, applying any Lv. 25 milestone on the way; Prodigy Fruit makes the next milestone grant both outcomes. Power Stone work is not an item: the Stone Workshop below Special Items handles it. Bountiful Berries and Empty Leftover Jars go in Cooking special slots, the Fortune and Challenger's Charms are toggled on level select, and Treasure Keys drop on their own roll after any victory (12% on a regular win, 25% on a Boss level, 35% with Fortune) and open the locked cache that every Boss level, and about 45% of regular levels (fixed per node by its seed), hides in a side pocket: walk a team member onto it with a key in hand for a Power Stone rolled with Fortune-rate bonuses, two ingredient bundles, and a Boss-rate chance at another special item. Special items drop only from victories and are rare: a 3% chance on an ordinary win, 8% on a Boss level, and 48% while a Fortune Charm is active, with the item drawn from a weighted table in `GameData.SPECIAL_ITEM_DROP_WEIGHTS` that covers every special item except Treasure Keys, including Bountiful Berries, Empty Leftover Jars, and the Fortune, Challenger's, Health, and Attack Charms.

Expedition terrain follows a soft-meadow concept (a smoothly shaded rolling world, not a tile world): the gameplay grid of walkable cells, rivers, and walls still decides where Quiblets can go, but the ground you see is one continuous, smoothly shaded heightfield mesh (`build_terrain` in `scripts/expedition_3d.gd`, three samples per cell). Open ground rolls in broad swells (`PLAIN_ROLL`, a few long sine waves; fighters, props, patches, and the cache are seated on the surface every frame), blocked cells rise into rounded hills whose height grows with the distance from the nearest open ground (neighbouring wall tiers blended, thin walls capped lower), every hill spreads out through a skirt that starts at `HILL_SKIRT_SHARE` of its height and fades over `HILL_SKIRT_RADIUS` into the ground around it, and rivers sink into wide soft troughs inside their own cells. Every island keeps exactly its own palette (`GameData.EXPEDITION_BIOMES`: ground, path, cliff, and accent), with nothing pulled toward the meadow's greens: grass that brightens with height and takes an accent-tinted plateau colour on the highest ground, the path colour lining the troughs over a lightened cliff-toned bed under one smooth translucent water sheet (each vertex carries its flow, a phase, and the shore depth, so crests scroll downstream, the surface bobs and glints in the sun on rippled normals, and pale foam gathers along the shallow banks; waterfalls fall as the same water flowing straight down), a darker track along the trail, and a faint long-wave noise breaking the flat colour; nothing carries a texture and everything shades matte. Hills are terrain, not walls: every hill is walkable and Quiblets simply climb over it, so only rivers ever block a route. Every crossing carries an arched wooden bridge (curved planks, rails, posts, and knobs) planned before the terrain is built: its axis is whichever of the two orientations lands both ends on dry ground, never in the water, the cells it spans stay part of the channel so the river runs on continuously beneath it, and the deck is what Quiblets walk on (`deck_height_at`). About half of all rivers begin as a waterfall: the channel runs back high through the border hills and drops over the field's edge in a translucent sheet with foam and streaks at its foot. Props and hill decoration are smooth matte pieces: a tree is a tapered trunk under five round leaf blobs in four greens over a faint shadow disc, a bush three blobs, a boulder a tight cluster of three squashed grey blobs each seated on the ground under it, mushrooms a cylinder stem and a blob cap, pillars and cacti tapered cylinders, and a few puffy clouds of overlapping spheres drift high over the field. Lighting is soft meadow: a pale sky-blue ambient fill, one warm gentle sun with no shadows, no glow, linear tone mapping, and no depth haze. Rivers run mostly straight with the occasional one-tile jog. Expedition maps are big open fields in the style of Pokémon Quest: a regular level is 52×36 tiles, a Boss level 60×40, and a Berry Grove 64×36. Sunken rivers meander across the field: each biome's own channels run top to bottom, and one to three extra rivers are added at random per level, some running left to right. The water flows: a shader scrolls crests along each river's direction and bobs the surface, and the pale ripple slivers drift downstream. Rivers are three or four tiles wide and proper troughs, sunk 1.3 tiles below the plain with dark beds and translucent glossy water with pale ripples, and where a channel passes through a clearing only its middle stays open as a narrow bridged crossing so the river stays continuous; they cannot be crossed except at those bridges (one wherever the trail crosses a river, plus the occasional extra), and rounded walkable hills stand in the open, few on plains-like islands and many on walled-in ones like Glittergut Cave (each biome carries a `walls` openness value and a `rivers` count in `GameData.EXPEDITION_BIOMES`). The team starts at the left edge; three boss arenas zigzag across the field. Enemies come in sets: a field has eight spawn areas at random open spots re-rolled on every expedition, and each set (four to six per level, seven to nine per Boss level) appears at an unused spawn area away from the team only after the previous set is beaten. Every biome's prop kinds stand in the field as cube-built props (trees, boulders, big and small mushrooms, crystals, pillars, cacti, snow mounds, ruin blocks, by biome density), on the open plain and standing on the hills too; each blocks its tile. All of them, boulders included, are harvestable: a team member standing beside one for 2.5 seconds gathers two bundles of four to eight ingredients drawn from what that prop grows (fruit and leaves from trees, fungi from mushrooms, seeds and hard things from crystals, roots and earthy things from mounds, pillars, and blocks), slower but far richer than a berry patch, and the prop then breaks apart. Any prop can also be broken by a move that reaches it, which drops only a small bundle (area, line, cone, or a projectile stopped against it), bursting into a scatter of small cubes that fall, bounce, shrink, and vanish after a few seconds while the tile opens up again. When a set spawns the camera pans to it just until its last enemy has landed plus a short linger, and each enemy pops in just above its spot, hops up a little, and drops to the ground with a small squash on landing. A set idles until the team comes close, and the team auto-explores toward it along walkable ground until the player clicks elsewhere. When the last set falls, the boss and its escorts appear in whichever arena is nearest the team: the camera pans over, and a couple of seconds later the boss turns to face the camera, lets out a synthesised "errr… grrnt", and stretches up during the first half of it and squashes down during the second. Chasing a target is routed the same way whenever the straight line is blocked by a river, wall, or prop, re-planned a few times a second and cut short once the chaser has a clear line within its attack reach, for enemies as well as the team, and never overriding a player command. Every commanded run, including auto-exploration, is routed over the walkable tile grid (a breadth-first search that is then string-pulled), so Quiblets thread corridor gaps and reach berry patches tucked into side pockets instead of pushing into cliffs. While following a route the corner steering is off (the route already keeps clear of cliffs, and steering against it is what used to leave Quiblets juddering beside walls), movement eases in over the last stretch so arrivals settle, a final point counts as reached when a crowd of teammates keeps anyone from the exact spot, and any Quiblet that stops gaining ground for 0.7 seconds asks the expedition for a fresh route to wherever it was heading (the rest of its command, or its chase target). A moving Quiblet turns smoothly to face the way it is heading on every kind of movement (routes, chases, kiting, retreats); commanded runs used to leave the model facing wherever it last fought. Regression check `res://tests/movement_stuck.gd`, which steps physics by hand and fails against the old rules. Any ground standing between the camera and a Quiblet or enemy fades smoothly to 20% opacity and fades back once nothing is hidden behind it: the expedition marches each living fighter's line up to the camera through the heightfield, ramps a per-fighter weight over about a fifth of a second, and the terrain shader fades only the fragments lying on those lines (a soft-edged tube around each line, above the line's height so the ground at a fighter's feet stays solid); the terrain draws in two passes, an opaque one that cuts a hole along each faded line and a transparent next pass that fills the hole at 20%, so the water in the troughs is never overdrawn. Regression check `res://tests/wall_fade.gd`, visual check `res://tests/wall_fade_visual.gd`.

A move plays out in full once it starts, whatever the enemies do in the meantime: a stun, bubble, cocoon, or launch landing on the user no longer cuts a dash or beam short, Echo and Link follow-ups fire on schedule through those effects (they only wait for the user's own in-progress dash or beam), and a projectile or area effect already in flight still lands if the user is knocked out. A follow-up whose target has died retargets only to an enemy within the move's reach; with none there it still lands on the original target if it lives, else on the spot the move was aimed at, so an Echo never vanishes or flies across the map to a far-off set. Only a knocked-out user's own body-driven dash or beam stops, and nothing new can be started while stunned. Regression check `res://tests/moves_runtime.gd`. Berry Groves are wider fields with five meadows and dozens of patches (26 in the Berry Grove, 34 in the optional one) tucked into off-trail pockets, about 60% of them berries and the rest any resource, while the four patches in a regular or Boss level roll from the whole ingredient list; every one of the sixteen ingredients has its own plant shape (a pair of purple berries, a teal melon on a stem, a fan of bitter leaves, upright red pepper pods, a grey bean pod, a glowing corn cob, and so on) so the likely reward is readable from afar, and the team is led on to the next meadow once the ones behind it are picked clean; Boss levels use larger fields, more enemy sets, and three large boss arenas. The map is carved from a one-unit tile grid for gameplay: walkable tiles form the plain with a worn sandy trail along the route, and everything off the route rises into rounded hills, and the same harvestable props stand on the open ground and on the hills alike. Every island has its own biome palette and decoration kind in `GameData.EXPEDITION_BIOMES` (trees, rocks, crystals, pillars, cacti, mushrooms, or mounds), and wet biomes form pools along the cliffs. Layouts are seeded by island and route node, so a level looks the same every visit. Regression check: `res://tests/expedition_maps.gd`.

Difficulty scales three ways. Enemy HP and damage multipliers rise by island, from 68% HP and 58% damage on Longgrass Fields through parity around the sixth island to 155% HP and 140% damage on the last, and islands seven and thirteen onward add one and two extra enemies per wave. Damage between the two sides is scaled by the level gap, 3.5% per level of difference clamped between 0.35× and 2.5×, so an under-levelled team both deals less and takes more. From the fourth island onward enemies also answer the team's own growth: when the team's average level is above the island's stage level, enemies close a share of that lead (20% on island four, 45% around the sixth, 70% on the eleventh, 90% on the last), and they carry a share of the team's fitted Power Stone strength as flat HP and Attack (25% rising to 110%), measured as the average of the team's mean and its strongest member so one heavily stoned carry is not diluted. The first three islands apply neither. Team Quiblets below full health naturally recover 1% of their max HP per second during an expedition. Knocked-out Quiblets revive after 10 seconds at half health, as many times as needed; the expedition is lost only when the whole team is down at once. The level-route cards rate each node from the real matchup formula. Anchors and constants live in `scripts/game_data.gd`; regression check `res://tests/difficulty.gd`.

Every expedition outcome—victory, failure, or Give Up—opens two result screens. The first shows every participating Quiblet as a scaled copy of its full Quiblet info menu (the info section above its stone equipment menu with moves, Move Stone slots, fitted stones, and all sixteen Power Stone slots) and animates the info section from its pre-expedition XP, level, stats, and species to its final state, with no written change summary. The Power Stone grid starts at the pre-expedition state too: the unlock square sweeps as XP rises, and crossing an unlock level opens that slot and moves the square to the following one. Each card is condensed (info strip above the move list with the grid beside it) and scaled as large as the screen allows for the team size; input remains locked until all animations finish. The next click opens a complete haul grid containing ingredients, Move Stones, Power Stones, and special items. A final click returns to the island selector.

Music is state-driven, and whatever track is playing carries on unchanged under the expedition pause menu. `BaseCamp.wav` fades in from silence after the startup title, then loops continuously throughout Base Camp and its Resources, Cooking, Recipes, Spices, Training, and Quiblet-management menus without restarting between them. Starting a valid stew plays `StartedCooking.wav` once over the continuing Base Camp track as you return to camp. Moving between Base Camp and expedition selection performs a gradual two-track crossfade in either direction. `Expedition.wav` then loops continuously across both island and level selection and into ordinary expedition play. `BerryGrove.wav` loops in both Berry Grove types, and `BossTheme.wav` takes over for level bosses and Boss levels. The exact track active at expedition completion continues uninterrupted through both the Quiblet-update and acquired-items screens; entering island selection afterward restores the regular Expedition theme. Defeating a level boss, in a regular level or a Boss level, routs every remaining enemy of that wave (each drops the loot it would have dropped) and the Boss theme keeps playing through the results screens. `GotQuiblet.wav` and `GotQuibletSpecial.wav` are one-shot arrival themes, with the special version used only when an actually rare Quiblet arrives. `StartupTheme.wav` is a one-shot synchronized with the opening QUIBLETS title reveal and plays without overlapping the Base Camp track.

Stews receive a hidden 0–100 score from ingredient tier (60 points), recipe precision (30 points), and ingredient variety (10 points). The results are Decent, Good, Great, and Amazing. Quality controls arrival level, rare-pool weighting, starting Power Stone chance, and cooking duration: Decent takes 1 finished expedition, Good takes 2, Great takes 3, and Amazing takes 5. Victory, defeat, and Give Up all advance the pot once. Spices independently bias which eligible Quiblet arrives and permanently season each arrival with a small stat bonus (for example Hot Flakes add Attack, Iron Flakes add max HP, Swift Spice adds movement speed, and Brain Salt trims move cooldowns), scaled by spice quality and stacking with Power Stone bonuses; Bountiful Berries only increase the number of arrivals; matching leftovers and Empty Leftover Jars collect leftovers and improve recipe precision. Unfitted Power Stones can be recycled from the stone detail panel on the Quiblet menu for one ingredient per tier plus one per bonus stat plus one, rolled at the level the stone's tier corresponds to; a confirmation dialog that names the stone, its bonuses, and the payout appears first, since recycling destroys the stone. Filled leftover jars are listed per recipe under Resources beside the special items and on the Recipe Journal cards; either place can recycle one for two to four ingredients that fit the dish, and the cook result screen says where a fresh jar went.

Ingredient drops from defeated enemies and berry patches are weighted by ingredient tier against the stage level. Each tier has a stage level where it drops at full weight (tier 1 always, tier 2 from level 6, tier 3 from 14, tier 4 from 24); below that, its weight fades over six levels to a small floor, so rare ingredients like Frostberry and Sunplum stay possible early but uncommon. These thresholds live in `scripts/game_data.gd` and are initial balance values. Regression check: `res://tests/ingredient_drops.gd`.

The owned Quiblets list is sorted by level, highest first, with Quiblets on the same level in order of arrival. The stone list on the Quiblet info menu and the Stone Workshop show every owned Power Stone in one order (Health before Attack, stronger first), including stones fitted on any Quiblet, which sit in their natural place drawn darker with the owner's portrait in the top-right corner; a fitted stone can be inspected but not dragged, recycled, or used in the workshop until it is taken off. The list's cards stretch to fill the panel's width. Empty Move Stone slots can be dragged from one move to another on the Quiblet info menu (drop on the other move's icon or any of its slots): the slot leaves the source move and joins the target, up to eight slots per move; a slot with a stone fitted stays where it is, and the move can only give away slots that are empty. Clicking a fitted Power Stone or Move Stone on the Quiblet info menu selects it in the stone detail panel, and clicking an ingredient, spice, or special item placed in a pot slot shows it in the Cooking info panel.

The Cooking screen's recipe browser shows one stew at a time with up and down arrows to step through all eighteen (wrapping at the ends): the stew's number in the top-left corner, its name, the ingredient combination it requires, and to the right a generic hint of what it attracts (a type, colour, or build, never the exact species). Undiscovered stews show \"???\" for all three until cooked once.

Every ingredient begins undiscovered and unlocks when it is first collected. Cooking only lists owned spices and special ingredients. Any occupied ingredient, spice, or special-ingredient slot can be dragged out to refund it, then dropped into a compatible slot again. The pot art's lid shows "Predicted Result: —" with the stew the current pot would make, "???" if that stew hasn't been discovered yet, or "---" with fewer than five ingredients; the start-cooking confirmation deliberately does not name the stew. Confirming returns you straight to Base Camp, where the lid appears high above the pot, hops slightly, then spins quickly as it drops squarely onto the rim. Leaving Cooking with anything placed in the pot requires confirmation; confirming returns every reserved item to inventory.

## Training

The **TRAIN** button beside the Quiblet info section opens Training, which replaces the team preview while keeping the info section and the Quiblet list. Pick **Move Training** or **EXP Training** at the top, drag a Quiblet into the trainee slot, up to four into the helper slots, and one owned ingredient from the two-by-eight ingredient grid into the food slot, then press **TRAIN**. Right-click any socket to clear it. The food is spent whether or not the session succeeds.

Training consumes its helpers, so the TRAIN button opens a confirmation first that names the trainee, what the training does, every helper with its chance to stay, and the food used. Drag a Quiblet into the trainee slot, up to four helpers into the helper slots, and up to two ingredients into the food slots, then press **TRAIN**. Every helper is spent unless it passes a preservation roll: each added ingredient adds rarity × compatibility (T1 2.5/5/10%, T2 5/10/20%, T3 7.5/15/30%, T4 10/20/40% for poor, neutral, and matching food), and a preserved helper still gives its full contribution. In Move Training the section lists the trainee's moves with their Move Stone slots and fitted stones; click one to choose which move gets retrained. Success starts at 5% and adds the strongest relationship bonus per helper (+25% same species, +20% same evolution family, +12% same type, +5% unrelated), capped at 95%. On failure the move is unchanged; on success it is replaced by a weighted pick from the learnset excluding every move already in a slot, with later learnset moves rarer, its slot count kept and fitted stones refunded. EXP Training always succeeds and awards 30% of each helper's lifetime EXP times its relationship multiplier (×1.75 species, ×1.5 family, ×1.2 type, ×1.0 unrelated). Constants live in `scripts/game_data.gd`; regression check `res://tests/training.gd`.

## Move behavior

All 63 moves have explicit gameplay profiles in `scripts/move_behaviors.gd`; actual collisions, delivery, and visuals run together in `scripts/move_cast_3d.gd`. Projectiles can miss or collide with terrain. Dashes sweep their paths. Beams, cones, piercing lines, moving waves, delayed impacts, persistent areas, and proximity mines use their own hit shapes and timing.

Quiblets have timed Burn, leech, roots, bubbles, disabling spores, shields, thorns, growth, smoke, evasion, and cocoon states. Ignite applies Burn; Combust consumes it. Healing Bloom heals periodically, Pollen Puff heals immediately, and Cocoon prevents movement and other actions while healing. Last Bloom requires 35% HP or less. These durations, strengths, and thresholds are initial balance values that can be tuned in the profiles.

Move Stone compatibility follows these actual behaviors. Echo repeats only after the entire cast finishes, with a cooldown penalty but no strength penalty. Rush reduces cooldown without reducing strength. Link uses the destination's stones, ignores its cooldown, and cannot loop. Drain heals from actual HP damage dealt, including its applicable damage-over-time effects. Pausing freezes active casts; knockout cancels the user's active casts and pending follow-ups.

Gameplay regression checks: run Godot headless with `--path . --script res://tests/moves.gd` and `res://tests/moves_runtime.gd`. These exercise all 63 moves, spatial/timing behavior, status interactions, stone modifiers, live expedition physics, pausing, and freed targets. `res://tests/moves_visual.gd` renders a six-move visual check using the actual runtime.

## Move Stones

Each defeated enemy first rolls its stone-drop chance (46.8% by default, overridable with `stone_drop_chance` in that enemy's data). A successful roll awards exactly one stone: 96% Power Stone or 4% Move Stone. Ingredient drops are independent.

Every Quiblet rolls one fixed 4×4 Power Stone board when it is created, and the board never changes. Each position is Attack, Health, or Flex (45% / 45% / 10%, with at least one Attack and one Health guaranteed); Flex accepts either stone type. Each position also has a unique unlock level: one at Lv 1, two from Lv 2–10, three from 11–25, three from 26–45, three from 46–65, two from 66–82, one from 83–94, and one from 95–100, shuffled so position says nothing about unlock order. Slots at or below the Quiblet's level are unlocked; the next slot to unlock shows a circular progress ring with progress = (level − previous unlock level) ÷ (next unlock level − previous unlock level), and all later slots stay locked, drawn at half size with the grey Locked art and no level tag or tooltip. Every slot type has its own icon, including the split Attack/Health mark on Flex slots (`FlexSlot.png` and `FlexSlotLocked.png` in `textures/UI`). Quiblets from older saves roll their board from their identity on load, and any stone left in a locked or mismatched slot returns to inventory. The bands live in `POWER_BOARD_UNLOCK_BANDS` in `scripts/game_data.gd`; regression check `res://tests/power_board.gd`.

Power Stones of Bronze quality and above carry one to four bonus stats, and each bonus has one exact, fixed effect that the stone's detail panel and tooltip spell out: Health +5% max HP, Attack +5% Attack, Critical Hit Rate +8% chance for a 1.5× hit, Movement Speed +10%, Move Cooldown −6%, Damage Resistance −5% damage taken, Healing Received +10%, Knockback Resistance −25% knockback and pull distance, Evasion +4% chance to dodge a move. Bonuses on equipped stones stack additively per stat. Dropped stones carry bonuses with an 18% (one), 6% (two), 2% (three), or 0.5% (four) chance, doubled by a Fortune Charm. A stone's material follows its number of distinct bonus stats: Regular (0), Bronze (1), Silver (2), Gold (3), Diamond (4), Obsidian (5 or more); a bonus line that holds several rolls of one stat shows as one line with the added-together amount (`Critical Hit Rate: +16% chance for a critical hit`), and its effect is exactly that amount. Two caps keep stacking honest: a stat holds at most 3 rolls on one stone (`MAX_BONUS_STACKS`; rolls beyond it are lost when combining, and stones saved with more are clamped on load), and each stat's total across a Quiblet's equipped stones is capped (`POWER_STONE_BONUS_CAPS`: max HP and Attack 60%, crit 50%, speed 60%, cooldown 50%, damage resistance 40%, healing and knockback resistance 100%, evasion 30%). The catalog is `POWER_STONE_BONUSES` in `scripts/game_data.gd`; regression check `res://tests/power_stone_bonuses.gd`.

The Stone Workshop (opened from Resources) reworks unfitted inventory Power Stones; nothing is spent but the stones themselves. Combiner: pick 2–4 stones of one stat type that each carry at least one bonus, with at most one Obsidian among them; the result keeps the lowest input power (so an ancient 118-power stone drags a 410-power stone down to 118), gains every bonus with matching stats added together, takes its material from its distinct stats (2 Silver, 3 Gold, 4 Diamond, 5+ Obsidian, the only source of Obsidian), and consumes every input; the preview says how many rolls the per-stat stack cap discards. Revitalizer: a stone's power becomes max(current power, round(0.9 × average drop power at the loot tier of the highest node reached)), keeping everything else. Converter: flips a Health stone to Attack or an Attack stone to Health with its power and bonuses untouched. Reforger: choose one bonus line on a stone and consume a second stone that has a bonus; the chosen line is replaced with a fresh random stat the stone does not already carry. The rules are static functions in `scripts/game_data.gd` (`combine_power_stones`, `revitalize_power_stone`, `convert_power_stone`, `reforge_power_stone`); regression check `res://tests/stone_workshop.gd`, visual check `res://tests/workshop_visual.gd`.

Open **Team & Moves**, select a Quiblet, and press **＋** beside a move to fit a stone. The picker includes Echo, Heavy, Reach, Chain, Sharing, Split, Rush, Seeking, Lingering, Blast, Force, Drain, and Link using the textures in `res://textures/MoveStones`. Right-click a fitted icon to remove it.

Link asks for a destination move and occupies a slot on both moves. Linked moves ignore their own cooldown when triggered, can form chains, and cannot revisit a move already used in the same chain.

Models, textures, and audio live in the project's asset folders; move effects use procedural 3D geometry.
